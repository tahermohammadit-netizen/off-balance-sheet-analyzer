How the rules work
  • Text is split into passages. A passage is flagged when it contains a keyword pattern for a topic (shown underlined).
  • Amounts are extracted only when written with $, US$ or USD, or as “N million/billion dollars”. They convert to USD billions only when the unit is written in the same passage. Otherwise they are shown as written.
  • Classification checks cues in this order: guarantee or contingency wording (contingent exposure), commitment or “not recorded” wording (off-balance-sheet commitment), equity-interest wording (equity investment), recorded or debt wording (reported liability), then “not consolidated” wording (off-balance-sheet). If no cue matches, the passage is marked for manual review.
  • Guarantees and commitments are never added to debt automatically. You can tick an amount to test it as hypothetical debt.

These are keyword rules, not accounting judgement. Always read the passage and the full footnote.

ASC 810: the VIE consolidation test

Whether Hyperion is consolidated depends on a two-step analysis. Open each step. This is a general summary of the framework, not a conclusion about Meta’s actual accounting.

Step 1: Is the entity a variable interest entity (VIE)?

An entity is a VIE if any one of these holds:

  • Its equity at risk is not enough to finance its activities without extra subordinated support.
  • Equity holders as a group lack the power to direct the activities that most affect performance, or lack the obligation to absorb losses or the right to receive returns.
  • Voting rights are disproportionate to economic interests, and substantially all activities involve or are conducted for the investor with fewer voting rights.
Step 2: Is Meta the primary beneficiary?

The primary beneficiary consolidates the VIE. It must have both:

  • Power to direct the activities that most significantly affect the VIE’s economic performance.
  • Economics: the obligation to absorb losses, or the right to receive benefits, that could be significant to the VIE.

Both conditions are required. If another party meets both, that party consolidates instead.

What changes if Meta consolidates
  • The VIE’s assets and liabilities, including its bonds, come onto Meta’s balance sheet.
  • Equity held by other investors appears as non-controlling interest.
  • Leverage ratios rise, which is what the hypothetical case above shows.

The case’s Hyperion figures ($27B bonds, $2.5B non-controlling interest) are used only to size the hypothetical adjustment.
